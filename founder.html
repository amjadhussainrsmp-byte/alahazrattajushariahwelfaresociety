A life shaped by community

From Bareilly, Uttar Pradesh, to wider public service initiatives.

For Dr Farmaan Hasan Khan, social service is a practical responsibility: to notice where people are struggling, bring the right people together, and help turn goodwill into meaningful support.

Born on 8 August 1995 in Bareilly, Uttar Pradesh, Farmaan Hasan Khan—popularly known as Farmaan Mian—grew up in a city with a rich religious, cultural and civic heritage. His published biography describes a family environment shaped by faith, compassion and public service. These influences are reflected in the causes he has chosen to support: access to healthcare, education for young people, opportunities for women, environmental awareness and social harmony.

He studied at Mahatma Jyotiba Phule Rohilkhand University in Bareilly and completed a B.A. LL.B. His educational background brings a legal and civic perspective to his public-facing work, while his community activities have placed him in contact with volunteers, medical professionals, educators and people seeking assistance.

Rather than limiting welfare to a single field, his public initiatives have brought different needs into the same conversation. Healthcare can help a family through an immediate crisis; education can expand a young person's future; digital skills can create new choices; and environmental work can build a shared sense of responsibility for the places people live.

“Faith and values find their fullest expression when they inspire care, justice, learning and service to others.”

This profile draws on the founder's published biography and public reporting. It focuses on documented roles and initiatives, rather than making assumptions about private life or attributing unverified claims to him.

A continuing vision

Make service practical, inclusive and sustained.

The long-term vision associated with Farmaan Hasan Khan's work is to make community service a continuing practice rather than an occasional gesture. It means bringing support closer to people, encouraging young people to learn and participate, and building partnerships that can respond to local needs.

For a welfare organisation, the work is never complete after a single event. Lasting progress also depends on listening to communities, following up where possible, using resources responsibly and making sure the people being served remain at the centre of decisions.

Public profile and recognition

Work that has drawn public attention.

Farmaan Hasan Khan's social initiatives have received media coverage, including reporting on welfare programmes conducted around the annual Urs-e-Ala Hazrat in Bareilly. His published biography also records the Bharat Gaurav Ratna recognition in 2023.

Recognition can bring visibility to a cause, but the lasting measure of community work is whether initiatives are useful, responsibly delivered and responsive to people's needs.
